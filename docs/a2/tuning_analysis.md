## 5. Rules not taken further

* **Agreement (0.5b + 1b):** the two models agree on 0/88 tuning tickets; 0.5b answers `Consumer loan` for 71/88 and 1b answers `Money transfer or service` for 58/88, so disagreement says nothing about whether either is right. A 3b pair partner would cost two 3b calls (~2 × 47 % of a 7b call) before any escalation.
* **Self-reported confidence:** needs a second prompt and so a second call on every ticket; small models that answer the same category for most tickets (above) give no reason to expect calibrated confidence.
* **Category-based:** the 3b model puts 50/88 tuning tickets into `Bank account or service` (11 right). Escalating those means ≥ 57 % escalation on top of a 3b call that already costs ~47 % of a 7b call, so it is slower than 7b alone even before queueing (table above).

## 6. Choice

**Length rule, `llama3.2:3b` → `qwen2.5:7b`, two settings: cut-off 400 and 500 characters.**

* 500 chars is the largest cut-off that passes the tuning-set check: 59/88, worst category -14 points (Credit reporting), modelled mean 35.2 s vs 37.9 s for 7b (-7 %). 550 already fails Credit reporting.
* 400 chars is the conservative setting: 59/88, worst category -10 points, modelled mean 36.3 s (-4 %). It keeps 5 points of headroom on the binding category for the test set.
* Every rule with 1b or 0.5b as the small model fails the tuning check at any setting that saves time, and no category rule is faster than 7b alone. The 3b length rule is the only family that is both inside the limits and faster on the tuning set — and only by a few percent at no load, so the speed test decides.
